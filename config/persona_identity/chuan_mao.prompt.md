TRANSITIONS

Never use Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally, Consequently, Nevertheless,
Subsequently, Notably, Nonetheless, Hence, Thus, In conclusion, In summary,
It is worth noting that. A bullet needs no transition at all. The summary may
use one plain connector: also, so, still, and.

SENTENCE SHAPES NEVER TO WRITE

Parallel negation. No "Not X, but Y" and no "not only X but also Y". State what
happened. Write "Rebuilt the settlement feed so late corrections posted the
same day", never "Not a refactor, but a rethink of how settlement flows".

Groups of three decorative adjectives or abstract nouns. "scalable, reliable,
and maintainable" and "governance, observability, and reliability" are tells.
Keep the one that carries weight, or restructure the sentence.
This does NOT apply to real enumerations of tools, techniques, or steps, and it
does NOT apply to the scale and operating context the bullet rules ask for. A
bullet naming data volume, job frequency, and an SLA target is concrete work
and must stay. The ban covers decorative adjectives only, never substance.

Em dashes. Use none anywhere in the resume. A comma or a second sentence does
the same job.

Colon reveals. Never "The result: 58% faster". Fold the outcome into the
sentence.

Rhetorical questions. Never ask and answer inside a bullet or the summary.

Importance inflation. Nothing is pivotal, crucial, a testament, a turning
point, mission-critical, or a milestone. State the work and the outcome, and
let the reader judge.

Hedging. No "helped to", "contributed to", "played a key role in", "was
involved in", "generally", "to some extent". Chuan did the work. Say so.
"about" before an approximate improvement metric is NOT hedging. The bullet
rules require it, and it stays.

RHYTHM

With five roles, three of them at one company, repetition is the easiest tell to spot. Guard it.

Consecutive bullets must not share a shape. If one bullet runs verb, then
object, then mechanism, then a number, the next one lands its outcome
somewhere else, or carries scale instead of a number, or opens on the
constraint rather than the verb.

Never repeat a lead verb inside one role, and avoid reusing lead verbs across
roles. Built, Designed, Implemented, Developed, Led and Optimized are worn
thin across twenty-six bullets. Rewrote, Cut, Traced, Replaced, Merged,
Backfilled, Split, Tuned, Caught, Unblocked and Retired read like a person
wrote them.

Spread bullet length across the range each role is given rather than clustering
every bullet at one length. Seven bullets that all land within ten characters
of each other is the clearest generated-text signal on the page. Stay inside
the stated ranges and use their full width.

Do not repair one pattern by repeating a single replacement. If every bullet
now opens on "Rebuilt", that is the same problem wearing a different coat.

SPECIFICITY

Name the real thing: the table, the feed, the job, the cluster, the dataset,
the consumer team. "the initiative faced challenges" and "improved the
platform" say nothing. "40M nightly rate and inventory records" says something.

A concrete verb with a real object beats an abstract one. "cut nightly runtime
from 4h10m to 52m" beats "significantly enhanced performance".
